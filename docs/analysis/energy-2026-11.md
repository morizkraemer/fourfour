# Track energy rating (1-10) - 2026-11

Opt-in energy rating in the Rust analysis (`fourfour-analysis-rs`), branch `r100-energy`. Off by default. No UI, no
library schema change, no USB writer change.

## What it is

`Config::energy` (default `false`) adds `Analysis::energy: Option<Energy>`. `Energy` holds `rating: u8` (1-10), the raw
`score`, the track-level features, and an 8 s window curve (`curve`, `windows`). `service::to_app_result` maps the rating
into the existing `AnalysisResult.energy: Option<u8>` (the library already has an `analyses.energy` column), so enabling
it in the app is one `cfg.energy = true`. The app's `Analyzer` still builds `Config::default()`, so nothing changes yet.

With the flag off the output is byte-identical (see below). With it on, `to_json` gains an `energy` key and `ms` gains
an `energy` timing; nothing else moves.

No Lexicon or Mixed In Key code, models or weights were used. Python `lexicon_energy.py` is a port of Lexicon's notes
and was not consulted. The design starts from the generic feature list in `experimentation-path.md` (spectral flux,
RMS, centroid, ZCR from our own earlier samplebase work) and the weights were fitted here.

## Method

Pure Rust on the decoded gapless mono (`src/energy.rs`), runs on its own thread next to the beat model, no new
dependency (`rustfft` is already used).

1. Box-decimate to about 22 kHz, normalise by the 99.9th percentile block peak (so features do not depend on file
   gain), Hann STFT 1024 / hop 512.
2. Per 8 s window (equal split of the track, about 8 s each) compute 8 features: `level_db` (window RMS vs track peak),
   `dynamics_db` (std of 23 ms frame loudness), `flux` (mean positive log-magnitude change, 30 Hz-5 kHz), `low_db`
   (30-150 Hz vs the rest), `high_db` (above 2 kHz), `centroid_log2`, `flatness_db` (spectral flatness 30 Hz-5 kHz),
   `onset_rate` (flux peaks per second).
3. Window score = sum of weight x standardised feature. Track score = mean of the top half of window scores (the loud
   sections define the rating; intro and breakdown do not drag it down).
4. Rating = 1 + number of the 9 cut points the score passes. Cut points are the dev-set deciles, so the scale is
   roughly uniform on the calibration corpus.

BPM and beat strength are deliberately not inputs. They need the beat model (so the rating would break with it and
inherit tempo octave errors), and on the only available label proxy tempo is a genre tell (see ablation), which would
make the fit look better without measuring energy. `Energy` therefore works with `Config::beats = false`.

Fitted weights (non-negative ridge, standardised window features): `level_db` 0.81, `low_db` 0.41, `flatness_db` 0.99.
All others fitted to 0 on the dev split (`dynamics_db`, `flux`, `high_db`, `centroid_log2`, `onset_rate`). They are still
computed and reported (cost is small, and a later fit on real labels may want them).

## Labels: there are none

Checked read-only: `benchmark/datasets/*`, the manifests, `rekordbox-testdata-2025.json`, the Rekordbox `master.db`
exports (no energy column in Rekordbox), file tags of the 1330 audio files (only an unrelated "Energy.mp3" cover string).
Beatport meta has genre, key, BPM, no energy. So there is no Mixed In Key or Lexicon rating to evaluate against and
**agreement (exact, +-1) against a human or tool energy rating could not be measured.**

## Proxy evaluation (and its limit)

Label proxy: an a priori genre energy table (1-10, written into `benchmark/scripts/energy_prep.py` before any result was
seen, e.g. Electronica/Downtempo 2, Deep House 4, House 6, Trance 7, Drum and Bass 8, Hard Dance 9) applied to the
Beatport EDM key dataset (697 two-minute 96 kbps previews, genre from `Beatport-EDM-Key-Dataset.xlsx`). Split by md5 of
the track id: 348 dev (fit weights, cut points, ridge strength by 5-fold CV), 349 held-out test (reported below, never
used for fitting). 95 % intervals are bootstrap over tracks.

| Held-out (n=349), vs genre prior | Spearman | pair AUC (prior gap >= 4) |
|---|---|---|
| a priori fusion weights (flux .30, centroid .15, level .20, onset .10) | 0.135 [0.03, 0.25] | 0.663 |
| ridge, unconstrained | 0.475 [0.39, 0.56] | 0.797 |
| **ridge, non-negative (shipped)** | **0.476 [0.40, 0.56]** | **0.791 [0.73, 0.85]** |
| shipped model without `dynamics_db` and `flatness_db` | 0.442 | 0.792 |
| shipped model + Rekordbox BPM as a feature (not shipped) | 0.585 | 0.816 |

Rating-vs-prior Spearman on held-out: 0.479. Window aggregation (held-out Spearman): all windows 0.365, top 50 %
0.476 (used), top 25 % 0.489. Dev (training) Spearman 0.56, so the fit does not collapse out of sample.

Held-out mean rating by genre: Electronica/Downtempo 4.1, Deep House 3.6, Hip-Hop 4.1, Minimal 3.6, Tech House 4.9,
Techno 4.6, House 5.6, Breaks 6.4, Dubstep 6.5, Psy-Trance 6.5, Drum and Bass 6.7, Progressive 6.8, Electro House 7.2,
Trance 7.9, Hard Dance 8.7. Order is right at the ends; the middle is noisy (Techno and Tech House sit low, Breaks
high). Downtempo at 4.1 is higher than it should be.

Other checks on the user's own library (`rekordbox-testdata-2025`, lossless and mp3, seeded samples):

- **Re-encode stability** (30 lossless tracks to mp3 128k, aac 128k, and -6 dB gain): mp3 exact 17/30, within +-1
  30/30, mean abs diff 0.43, score Spearman 0.988; aac the same (0.983); gain -6 dB identical on 30/30. The movement is
  mostly `level_db` (codec overshoot moves the peak).
- **Section sanity** (153 full-length tracks, 3 min or more): the middle half of the energy curve is above both the
  first and last tenth in 125 (82 %). No label, but intros and outros being calmer is the expected shape.
- **Distribution**: ratings 1-10 on 159 library tracks: 34, 21, 24, 20, 25, 14, 15, 5, 1, 0. The cut points come from
  Beatport club previews, so a calmer library sits low (rating 9-10 is rare here).

What this does not show: within-genre ranking (the proxy has no within-genre truth), full-length tracks against
labels, anything about how DJs rate energy. The genre table is my judgement. Spearman about 0.48 against a crude proxy
is moderate evidence the ordering is sensible, not that it matches Mixed In Key.

The Rust ratings match the Python fit on all 697 tracks (0 rating mismatches, max score difference 4e-16), so the
constants in `energy.rs` are exactly the fitted ones.

Reproduce: `energy_prep.py` (lists, genre prior, split), `cargo run --release -p fourfour-analysis-rs --example
energy_dump -- --lean --no-beats --workers 2 --list ...` (windows to JSONL), `energy_eval.py fit|bpm|check|structure|
stability` (all in `benchmark/scripts/`, pure Python), `energy_reencode.py` (ffmpeg variants). `energy_eval.py rust`
prints the model constants for `energy.rs`.

## Cost

Stage timer `ms.energy` against the sum of all stage timers, full pipeline (beats, key, waveform, energy), one thread
per track, on a loaded shared box (load average 15-30, so absolute times are inflated):

- 4 full-length tracks (5.3 to 6.2 min): energy 0.48-0.67 s, 0.60-0.84 % of the pipeline.
- 6 two-minute clips: energy 0.17-0.35 s, 0.51-1.0 %.
- Whole-process CPU, same 4 tracks, flag off vs on: 319.6 s vs 327.6 s (+2.5 %), inside run-to-run noise on this box;
  the stage timer is the better number. Rough cost: about 0.1 s per minute of audio, about 1 % of the pipeline, almost
  all of it the beat model's share. With `beats = false` the pipeline is mostly decode, so energy is a larger share
  there, still well under a second per track.

It runs on a side thread in parallel with the beat model, so wall time on a multi-core run is about unchanged.

## Byte-identical with the flag off

`analyze-json` built from a clean `git archive` of `ov-1028` (8249974) vs the same binary built from this branch, 9
tracks (3 Beatport mp3, 2 wav, 2 flac, 2 mp3 from the library, `--threads 2`): output files identical (`cmp`, sha256
`830872d3...5709628`, 7.5 MB each, covers beats, BPM, key, color waveforms). The baseline build was done in a separate
directory so the shared target could not reuse these sources.

## Limits

- No human or tool energy labels exist in the repo data: rating quality is unvalidated against what DJs mean.
- Weights fit to a genre proxy: the model partly learns "genre-typical loudness, bass and density". Within-genre
  variation is unverified.
- Previews are 2 minute, 96 kbps, club-leaning; cut points inherit that. A calmer library gets low ratings.
- `level_db` depends on mastering (peak to RMS), not just on the music. Remasters and louder masters of the same song
  rate higher.
- Band-limited analysis (decimated to about 22 kHz, flux and flatness up to 5 kHz); very sparse or very dense
  full-spectrum content is only seen through that.
- Tracks shorter than 2 s or silent get no energy (`None`).
- BPM and beat strength are excluded by design; with Rekordbox BPM added the proxy score rises (0.585) but that is the
  genre confound, so it was not adopted.

## Next step that would settle it

Real labels. Cheapest: rate about 100 library tracks 1-10 on the phone (a review page), or export any Mixed In Key or
Lexicon ratings the owner already has and use them only as evaluation labels (never as training code). Then re-run
`energy_eval.py` with those labels, refit on a dev half, report agreement (exact, +-1, Spearman) on the other half, and
recalibrate the cut points on the user's own library.

## Where it could be carried (not done; `pioneer-usb-writer` is another lane)

Rekordbox and CDJ have no energy field. Options in the existing writer: the track comment string (`models::Track::comment`,
PDB comment and OneLibrary `djComment`) with the Mixed In Key style prefix (`"7 - ..."`), which MIK users already sort
by on CDJ; or the 0-5 star `rating` (PDB row byte, OneLibrary `content.rating`, currently written as 0), which is lossy
and collides with real star ratings. In our own library the value already has a home: `AnalysisResult.energy` and
`analyses.energy`.
